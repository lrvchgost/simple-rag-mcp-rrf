Selected passages (C. K. Scott Moncrieff translation, public domain;
for retrieval testing).

"The madeleine". She sent for one of those squat, plump little cakes called
"petites madeleines" which look as though they had been moulded in the fluted
valve of a scallop shell. ... No sooner had the warm liquid mixed with the
crumbs touched my palate than a shudder ran through me and I stopped,
intent upon the extraordinary thing that was happening to me. An exquisite
pleasure had invaded my senses ... immediately the old grey house upon the
street, where her room was, rose up like the scenery of a theatre.

"Vinteuil's sonata". And it was like the beginning of an unknown world ...
the little phrase, with its sustained, metallic, superannuated sweetness,
had just begun to weave its sonorous ribbon of sound.

"Sleep". For a long time I used to go to bed early. Sometimes, when I had put
out my candle, my eyes would close so quickly that I had not even time to say
"I'm going to sleep."

"The true voyage of discovery". The only true voyage of discovery ... would be
not to visit strange lands but to possess other eyes, to behold the universe
through the eyes of another, of a hundred others, to behold the hundred
universes that each of them beholds.

"The hour lays hold of men". People die in the hour to which they have grown
accustomed: Aunt Léonie at three o'clock, because three o'clock was the hour
of her arm-chair.

"Books". A beautiful book is not anything more than a kind of optical
instrument which the author has offered the reader: it is up to the reader
to learn to read himself in it.

"Albertine's death". I learned that she was dead, and understood that
nothing now could keep her from her travels: she was freer than the living.
