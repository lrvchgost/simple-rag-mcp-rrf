Key themes of «In Search of Lost Time» (study notes).

MEMORY. Two kinds: voluntary memory (a conscious, dull recollection) and
involuntary memory — the sudden return of the past through a sensation: the
taste of a madeleine soaked in tea (Combray), the sound of a spoon, the
unevenness of the paving-stones in the baptistery of St Mark's in Venice.
Involuntary memory returns not a fact but an entire hour of life.

TIME. Lost time is time wasted; regained time is reassembled from flashes.
The final Guermantes ball (the «bal de têtes») shows that people age at
different speeds, time is «propped up» like the panels of a mirror, and only
art can restore it.

JEALOUSY. Swann over Odette, the narrator over Gilberte and Albertine.
Jealousy is not information about the other but an optics in which the other
remains finally unknowable. The captivity of Albertine is an attempt to lock
the unknowable into an apartment on the Quai Conti.

SNOBISHNESS. Society is the second religion of the cycle. The Verdurins are
elevated, the Forchevilles dissolve into the Guermantes, and every rise of a
formerly «low» family wounds its former rulers. Snobbery is shown as a
mechanism of time: titles wear out, people do not.

ART. The only honest occupation: Vinteuil's sonata, Elstir's portraits,
Bergotte's book on Ruskin and, finally, the narrator's own book. The artist
must translate the flashes of involuntary memory into signs — otherwise time
wins.

LOVE. Always imaginary: we love a projection, not a person. That is why the
lover in the cycle is inevitably alone, and the death of the beloved only
completes the loneliness.
